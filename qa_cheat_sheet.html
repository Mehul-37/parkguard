JUDGES Q&A CHEAT SHEET

Anticipated Questions & Winning Answers for Round 2

🔧 Technical & Feasibility
Q: What happens if the internet goes down?
Winning Answer: "The system is built with an Offline-First Architecture. The local gateway buffers sensor data and transactions locally. Once connectivity is restored, it batch-syncs all hashes to the blockchain. Operations never stop."
Q: Isn't putting every transaction on Blockchain expensive/slow?
Winning Answer: "We don't mine a block for every 20-rupee ticket. We use Merkle Tree Batching. We aggregate 100+ transactions into a single hash root and commit that to the chain spread out over time. This keeps costs negligible and speed instant."
Q: Sensors (Ultrasonic/IR) are unreliable outdoors. How do you handle that?
Winning Answer: "Correct. That is why for street parking, we specify IP68-rated Road Stud Magnetometers (LoRaWAN). They detect magnetic disturbance of large metal objects (cars) and are immune to rain, dust, or light interference."
🚀 Operational & Adoption
Q: Contractors will disable the sensors or sabotage the system.
Winning Answer: "That is a feature, not a bug. If a sensor node goes 'offline' suddenly, the Audit Dashboard triggers a Red Alert for that specific sector. The municipality can instantly fine the contractor for 'Equipment Tampering' as per the SLA."
Q: Why not just use FastTag for everything?
Winning Answer: "FastTag is great for *payment*, but it doesn't solve *occupancy fraud*. A contractor can claim a lot is full when it's empty to surge prices, or claim it's empty when it's full (illegal parking). ParkGuard provides the Physical Verification layer that FastTag misses."
💰 Financial Viability
Q: This hardware is expensive. Is it viable for government?
Winning Answer: "The ROI is extremely fast. A single sensor costs ~₹2,400. Currently, pure cash leakage is ~30% (₹60/day/slot). By stopping this leakage, the hardware pays for itself in just 40 days. After that, it's pure profit for the state."
Q: Who pays for the system?
Winning Answer: "Ideally, a PPP Model (Public-Private Partnership). The Tech Partner (us) installs it. The revenue boost is shared. This removes the upfront CaPex burden from the Municipality."
🔮 Future Scope
Q: How does this scale to the whole city?
Winning Answer: "Since we use LoRaWAN, we don't need cabling. A single Gateway covers 2-3km radius. We can cover an entire district with just 5-6 gateways. The backend on Cloud scales horizontally."
